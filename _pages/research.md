Synthetic polymers are produced at enormous scale, with a growing need for more sustainable materials  is designed to be biodegradable. Whether a material actually degrades in the environment, and how quickly, depends on its chemical structure, the material's form, and the conditions it meets. My research quantifies these processes with analytical tools that can follow polymer carbon directly.

Macromolecular Analytical Chemistry {#analytical}
------
We work to develop new analytical approaches to study the breakdown of polymers in the environmental. Examples include <sup>13</sup>C labeling together with stable isotope probing (SIP) of CO<sub>2</sub> and microbial biomass during biodegradation, and NMR diffusion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) techniques to study macromolecular changes to polymers upon e.g., photoirradiation.

Environmental Fate of Consumer and Novel Plastics {#envfate}
------
At Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution I studied how sunlight degrades consumer plastics at the sea surface, and how formulation choices (additives such as titanium dioxide) and biofouling change that fate. This work continues through a U.S. National Science Foundation project on the controls of plastic photochemical reactivity in the surface ocean. It also led to new analytical tools, such as diffusion-ordered NMR spectroscopy for rapid determination of plastic molecular weight.
